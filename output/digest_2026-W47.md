#### 1. Once-Weekly Ribupatide for Weight Loss in Adults in China With Overweight or Obesity: The GEMINI-1 Randomized Clinical Trial.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-11-15
- 作者：Lin Zhao, Ying Chen, Zhifeng Cheng, Dan Zhu, Qifu Li, Hanqing Cai, et al.
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035098/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035098/)
- 命中关键词：obesity
- 摘要原文：Obesity is a major health problem in China; more pharmacotherapy choices for long-term weight management are needed. To evaluate the efficacy and safety of once-weekly subcutaneous ribupatide, a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide/glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, for weight loss in adults in China with overweight or obesity without diabetes. This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 clinical trial was conducted at 55 sites in China. From May to June 2024, adults without diabetes who had a body mass index of 28 or greater or 24 or greater with at least 1 weight-related comorbidity were enrolled, with final follow-up on June 20, 2025. Participants were randomized to receive once-weekly subcutaneous ribupatide (target doses of 2 mg, 4 mg, or 6 mg) or matching placebo for 48 weeks, with dose escalation for up to 20 weeks and concomitant lifestyle intervention. The primary outcome was change in body weight from baseline to week 48, including percent change in body weight and the percentage of participants achieving at least 5% body weight reduction, analyzed under the treatment policy estimand. Among 567 randomized participants (309 [54.5%] female; mean [SD] age was 34.4 [8.4] years, body weight was 93.0 [17.3] kg, and body mass index was 33.3 [4.5]), 514 (90.7%) completed the 48-week treatment period. Least-squares mean percentage changes in body weight at week 48 were -10.7%, -16.4%, and -17.7% with ribupatide 2, 4, and 6 mg, respectively, vs -1.4% with placebo. At week 48, the placebo-adjusted differences in percentage change in body weight were -9.3 (95% CI, -11.4 to -7.3) percentage points with ribupatide 2 mg, -15.0 (95% CI, -17.1 to -12.9) percentage points with ribupatide 4 mg, and -16.3 (95% CI, -18.4 to -14.2) percentage points with ribupatide 6 mg (P < .001 for all). For at least 5% body weight reduction at week 48, the multiply imputed response rates were 68.1% (95% CI, 59.9% to 75.3%) for ribupatide 2 mg (97 of 142), 88.0% (95% CI, 81.3% to 92.6%) for ribupatide 4 mg (124 of 141), 85.7% (95% CI, 78.6% to 90.7%) with ribupatide 6 mg (121 of 141), and 16.0% (95% CI, 10.8% to 23.1%) with placebo (23 of 143). Placebo-adjusted differences for ribupatide 2, 4, and 6 mg were 52.0 (95% CI, 42.2 to 61.9), 72.0 (95% CI, 63.7 to 80.2), and 69.7 (95% CI, 61.1 to 78.3) percentage points, respectively (all P < .001). The most frequent treatment-emergent adverse events with ribupatide were gastrointestinal events, occurring in 47.9%, 58.2%, and 63.1% of participants receiving ribupatide 2 mg, 4 mg, and 6 mg, respectively, compared with 23.1% receiving placebo; most were mild to moderate in severity and few participants (0.7%) permanently discontinued study treatment because of adverse events. Among adults in China with overweight or obesity without diabetes, once-weekly subcutaneous ribupatide resulted in significantly greater weight loss at 48 weeks. Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most frequent tolerability findings. ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06396429.

#### 3. Tirzepatide in Adolescents With Obesity: The SURMOUNT-ADOLESCENTS Randomized Clinical Trial.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-11-15
- 作者：Aaron S Kelly, Karthik Chandrasekhar Pamidipati, Marianna Rachmiel, Gitanjali Srivastava, Daniel S Hsia, Claudia K Fox, et al.
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035096/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035096/)
- 命中关键词：obesity, tirzepatide
- 摘要原文：Information on the effects of tirzepatide, a once-weekly glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist approved for obesity management in adults with obesity, remains limited in adolescents. To evaluate the efficacy and safety of once-weekly tirzepatide in adolescents with obesity or overweight with at least 1 obesity-related complication. This phase 3, randomized, double-blind, 72-week clinical trial was conducted at 31 medical research centers and hospitals in Canada, the Czech Republic, Israel, Italy, Poland, and the US from October 16, 2023, to June 2, 2026, among adolescents aged 12 years to younger than 18 years with obesity or overweight with at least 1 obesity-related complication. Participants were randomized 2:1 to receive once-weekly subcutaneous tirzepatide, escalated to a maximum tolerated dose (5, 10, or 15 mg), or placebo for 72 weeks, in addition to healthy lifestyle intervention. The primary end point was percent change in body mass index (BMI; calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared) from baseline to week 72. Key secondary end points included percentages of participants achieving BMI reductions of at least 5%, at least 10%, at least 15%, and at least 20%; changes in BMI SD score; BMI expressed as a percentage of the 95th percentile; and body weight, waist circumference, and physical functioning from baseline to week 72. Safety outcomes included treatment-emergent adverse events, serious adverse events, and discontinuations due to adverse events. A total of 160 participants were randomized to receive tirzepatide (n = 109) or placebo (n = 51). Mean (SD) age at baseline was 14.6 (1.6) years, 99 participants (61.9%) were female, mean body weight was 109.7 kg, and mean BMI was 38.6. At week 72, tirzepatide was associated with 18.5% reduction in BMI, compared with 0.5% increase in BMI with placebo, demonstrating superiority over placebo (estimated treatment difference, -19.0 [95% CI, -23.9 to -14.1] percentage points; P < .001). Of 10 key secondary outcomes, all demonstrated statistically significant differences favoring tirzepatide compared with placebo. Overall, 79.6% of tirzepatide-treated participants achieved BMI reduction of at least 5% compared with 28.5% of participants in the placebo group (risk difference, 51.2 [95% CI, 36.3-66.1] percentage points; P < .001). The most common adverse events with tirzepatide treatment were gastrointestinal (mostly mild to moderate). In adolescents with obesity or overweight with obesity-related complications, tirzepatide, in addition to healthy lifestyle intervention, led to significant reductions in percent change in BMI compared with placebo at 72 weeks. ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06075667.

#### 1. Smart insulin pens with integrated bolus calculator vs tracking-only connected insulin pens in adults with diabetes on multiple daily injections: a multicentre randomised controlled trial.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-11-14
- 作者：Sun-Joon Moon, Ji Yoon Kim, Jae Hyoung Cho, Ji Eun Jun, Sun Ok Song, Suk Chon, et al.
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034238/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034238/)
- 命中关键词：type 2 diabetes
- 摘要原文：Connected insulin pens (CIPs) have emerged as a promising tool for diabetes management with multiple daily injections (MDI), but evidence supporting the incremental benefit of integrated bolus calculator functionality (smart insulin pens [SIPs]) over tracking-only CIPs remains lacking. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of SIPs with bolus calculator compared with tracking-only CIPs in adults with diabetes on MDI. We hypothesised that SIPs with bolus calculator would improve glycaemic management compared with tracking-only CIPs. In this multicentre, open-label, randomised controlled trial conducted at nine medical centres in South Korea, we recruited adults aged 19-74 years with type 1 or type 2 diabetes on MDI therapy. Participants with HbA1c 58-108 mmol/mol (7.5-12.0%) were randomly assigned (1:1) using computer-generated block randomisation stratified by diabetes type (sequence generated by an independent statistician) to SIP with bolus calculator or tracking-only CIP for 12 weeks. All participants used continuous glucose monitoring (CGM). The primary outcome was the change in HbA1c from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included CGM metrics, insulin dosing behaviour and safety. A total of 120 participants were randomised to the SIP group (n=60) or the tracking-only CIP group (n=60); their mean (SD) age was 50.8 (14.8) years, 53 (44.2%) had type 1 diabetes and mean (SD) HbA1c was 69 (12) mmol/mol (8.5% [1.1%]). Of these, 116 were included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis. HbA1c decreased more with SIP than with tracking-only CIP (adjusted mean difference -3.39 mmol/mol [-0.31%]; 95% CI -6.45, -0.22 mmol/mol [-0.59%, -0.02%]; p=0.036). Time in range (3.9-10.0 mmol/l) improved significantly with SIP (57.2% vs 50.8%; adjusted mean difference 6.58%; 95% CI 1.52%, 11.64%; p=0.012). SIP users had fewer missed bolus doses (3.4 vs 3.7 times/day; p=0.020) and higher bolus frequency (3.2 vs 2.8 times/day; p=0.011). No severe hypoglycaemia or diabetic ketoacidosis occurred in either group. In this randomised controlled trial of adults with suboptimally controlled diabetes on MDI, SIP with bolus calculator significantly improved glycaemic management and dosing behaviour compared with tracking-only CIP. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06406439. This study was supported by G2E Co., Ltd, Korea and Huons Co., Ltd, Korea.
