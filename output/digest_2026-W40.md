### Nature Communications（1 -> 1）

#### 1. Reprogramming energy system for growth and bioproduction from CO₂ and methanol in Escherichia coli.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-09-26
- 作者：Kaifang Liu, Cong Gao, Guangjie Liang, Guipeng Hu, Wei Song, Wanqing Wei, et al.
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800808/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800808/)
- 命中关键词：metabolism
- 摘要原文：Microbial CO₂ assimilation provides a strategy for sustainable biomanufacturing. However, CO₂ reduction requires substantial energy input, while native energy systems are tightly regulated and extensively consumed by endogenous metabolism, limiting assimilation efficiency. Here, we design and construct an orthogonal energy system in Escherichia coli, achieving an intracellular NUDH concentration exceeding 7.0 mM and an NUDH/NUD⁺ ratio above 55. Subsequently, we design a CO₂-formate-acetyl-CoA-pyruvate-malate pathway and reprogram key enzymes for NUDH-dependent operation. Integration of the OES-driven CAM* pathway with a rationally rewired native electron transport chain supports growth of the engineered E. coli on CO₂ and methanol, with a doubling time of 8.6 ± 0.3 h and a maximum OD₆₀₀ of 37.5 ± 5.8. Moreover, the platform extends CO₂ assimilation to biosynthesis of multiple chemicals from distinct CAM* pathway nodes, including mevalonate, lactate, 2,3-butanediol, and succinate. Together, these results establish a framework for reprogramming microbial energy metabolism and expanding one-carbon biotransformation.

#### 1. The impact of physical activity and exercise timing on metabolic health among individuals at high risk of type 2 diabetes.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-09-26
- 作者：Soraya S de Kam, Tucker Reed, Femke Rutters, David J T Campbell, Parminder Raina, Renée de Mutsert, et al.
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42799754/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42799754/)
- 命中关键词：metabolic, obesity, type 2 diabetes
- 摘要原文：The timing of physical activity and exercise has been suggested to play a role in metabolic health. This narrative review synthesises current evidence on the impact of habitual physical activity and exercise timing on glucose homeostasis and insulin sensitivity, blood lipids and body composition among individuals with overweight, obesity and impaired glucose homeostasis. To this end, we reviewed evidence from both observational and experimental studies. Overall, current evidence on physical activity/exercise timing and metabolic health is inconsistent, with several studies reporting no significant time-dependent differences, and other studies suggesting more favourable glucose-related outcomes and improved insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles when physical activity/exercise is performed later in the day. Body composition outcomes appear to be largely independent of exercise timing. The interpretation of the current evidence is limited by the small number of studies, heterogenous study designs and variability in exercise modalities and durations. We identify important gaps in knowledge, particularly regarding sex-specific responses, the role of chronotype and the real-world feasibility of timing-based exercise recommendations. While engaging in physical activity or exercise at any time of day is more beneficial than remaining inactive, further studies are warranted to clarify the optimal timing for metabolic health in individuals at risk of type 2 diabetes.

### Kidney International（1 -> 1）

#### 1. Long-term outcomes of childhood nephrotic syndrome.

- 来源：PubMed
- 日期：2026-09-26
- 作者：Olivia Boyer, Aditi Sinha, Nestor Oliva-Dámaso, Andrew S Bomback, Evgenia Preka, Arvind Bagga
- 链接：[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800615/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42800615/)
- 命中关键词：obesity
- 摘要原文：Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome (INS) is the most common chronic glomerular disease in children. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, and the initial response, differentiating steroid-sensitive (SSNS) from steroid-resistant (SRNS) forms, is the strongest prognostic factor. While most childhood-onset INS results from an incompletely defined dysregulation of the immune system, up to one-third of children with initial SRNS have a monogenic etiology. SSNS typically follows a relapsing-remitting course, with one-half of the patients having frequent relapses or steroid-dependence (FRNS/SDNS). Although earlier studies suggested that most SSNS patients achieve sustained remission by puberty, approximately one-fourth experience at least one relapse in adulthood. Key predictors of persistent relapsing disease include FRNS/SDNS in childhood and the need for second-line immunosuppression. The disease may evolve into secondary steroid-resistance in <10% patients with SSNS. Kidney failure and death remain uncommon. In contrast, SRNS is associated with higher risks of therapy-and disease-related complications, including growth impairment, obesity, hypertension and cardiovascular risk, osteoporosis, cataracts, calcineurin inhibitor-induced nephrotoxicity, acute kidney injury and progressive chronic kidney disease. The risk of kidney failure is three- to fourfold higher in patients with monogenic and/or multidrug-resistant SRNS. This review synthesizes current evidence on the long-term outcomes of childhood-onset INS, highlights risk stratification across clinical phenotypes, genetic diagnosis and treatment response, and challenges during transition to adult care.
